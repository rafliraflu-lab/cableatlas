USB-C 60 W, 100 W and 240 W cable markings

Separate cable current limits from Power Delivery profiles.

Updated 18 November 2026 · By Saiful Bahri · Device-specific verification required

Worked example

A 20 V × 3 A profile represents 60 W; 20 V × 5 A represents 100 W. A 48 V × 5 A EPR profile represents 240 W. These are profile arithmetic, not charging test results. A legacy 100 W cable is not automatically EPR-capable.

Repeatable check

Write down the exact charger port output table and the laptop requested profile. Compare the cable’s documentation. USB-IF current compliant C-to-C labeling uses 60 W or 240 W markings; older cables may have legacy ratings. Higher voltage needs compatible EPR source, sink and cable.

Interpret the result

Do not use an unverified adapter to force higher voltage. If the charger cannot supply the needed common profile, choose a compliant combination instead.

Illustrative calculation or scenario, not an independent hardware test. Keep your own device records to verify the setup.
