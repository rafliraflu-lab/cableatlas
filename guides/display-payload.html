Display bandwidth: active pixels versus link rate

Estimate display data before buying a cable.

Updated 18 November 2026 · By Saiful Bahri · Device-specific verification required

Worked example

A 3840 × 2160 display at 120 Hz with 10 bits per RGB channel has 29.86 Gbit/s of active image data. That exceeds the 25.92 Gbit/s four-lane HBR3 payload ceiling even before blanking. At 60 Hz the active rate is 14.93 Gbit/s. Neither calculation establishes an actual supported mode.

Repeatable check

Start with the display manual’s supported timing table, then check the GPU output, dock lane allocation, cable rating and monitor input. If a mode fails, test the same resolution at 60 Hz first; keep color depth and other settings fixed. Re-enable refresh rate only after the base mode works. DSC can change the link budget but must be supported across the path.

Interpret the result

Do not compare raw HDMI link rates directly with DisplayPort payload. Use the calculator to screen out impossible uncompressed modes, not to promise compatibility.

Illustrative calculation or scenario, not an independent hardware test. Keep your own device records to verify the setup.
