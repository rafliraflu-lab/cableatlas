Why Some Adapters Work Only One Way

Learn why a USB-C-to-HDMI or HDMI-to-DisplayPort adapter is not always reversible.

Updated 11 November 2026 · CableAtlas editorial · Device-specific verification required

Key principle: A connection works only when the devices, ports, cables, and adapters support the required feature together. Always verify your specific device model.

Signal conversion is directional

A connector adapter may contain active electronics designed to convert a signal from a specific source to a specific display input.

Read source and destination labels

An adapter described as “DisplayPort source to HDMI display” should not be assumed to work with an HDMI source and DisplayPort monitor.

Power may be required

Some active converters require USB power. A missing power connection can make an otherwise correct setup fail.

Confirm the target mode

Check supported resolution, refresh rate, audio, HDR and other features, rather than relying on “4K” marketing alone.

Before purchase

Write down the source device port, destination device port, and desired mode. Search for an adapter explicitly supporting that direction.

WORKED EXAMPLE / WHAT TO CHECK

Example: a connection that looks correct but fails

A projector only has HDMI input, but a desktop PC has DisplayPort output. A user buys an HDMI-to-DisplayPort adapter because the connectors look correct, and nothing appears.

What is happening behind the connector?

Many adapters convert signals in one specific direction. Their electronic circuitry expects a source on one side and a sink on the other. A passive cable can also depend on a special source feature, while an active converter may need auxiliary USB power. Connector gender and the printed names are not enough; a listing must explicitly describe the direction, resolution and expected devices.

Reproduce the problem without buying anything

  1. Write down the source connector and destination connector in that order: “computer DisplayPort OUT → projector HDMI IN.”
  2. Compare that exact direction against the adapter’s manufacturer diagram and technical description.
  3. Check whether an active converter or extra power is required and whether the source supports passive mode.
  4. Test the projector with a known-working HDMI source first to eliminate an unrelated display-input problem.

How to decide what to replace

Search with the full direction and intended resolution rather than searching only two connector names. Keep the adapter’s model number and compatibility notes. If a unit is advertised as bidirectional, verify that the vendor explicitly supports both directions and the intended modes.

Primary reference: VESA DisplayPort FAQ. CableAtlas has not tested a particular brand or device; examples illustrate a method rather than a compatibility guarantee.

Verification checklist

  1. Find the exact model numbers of both devices.
  2. Read the official manufacturer specification for each port.
  3. Check the cable or adapter rating and required direction.
  4. Test a direct connection before adding hubs or docks.
  5. Record what worked so you can repeat the setup.
